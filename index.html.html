If your salt isMeasure

Non-iodized only. Iodine and the anti-caking agents in table salt hold back the lactic bacteria you're trying to feed. Polish rock salt (sól kamienna niejodowana), pickling salt and plain kosher salt all work.

Running the ferment

  • Everything stays under the liquid. A follower — a plate, a boiled stone, a water-filled bag, a cabbage leaf wedged under the shoulder of the jar — matters more than any number on this page.
  • Ferment at room temperature until it tastes right, then move it cold. Warmer is faster and softer; cooler is slower and crisper.
  • White film on top is kahm yeast. Skim it. Fuzzy, colored growth is mold: that jar goes out.
  • Chlorinated tap water slows the start. Let it sit out overnight or boil and cool it.
  • Topping a jar up later? Make the extra liquid at your target strength so you don't dilute what's already working.
Figures follow standard fermentation practice: salt as a share of the whole aqueous phase, which is what the bacteria actually sit in. Water contents are USDA averages and vary with the season and how long the vegetables sat in a cooler. Treat the output as a starting point and taste as you go.
